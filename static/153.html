The rule, in English

If you can change the rule after you see the number, the number is not evidence. These are the rules used here.

    How this could be nothing

    • John may have written 153 because it is a famous triangular number, and for no other reason.
    • The verse says “sons of Zebedee,” not “James and John.” That identification comes from earlier Gospels.
    • Two disciples in the boat are unnamed. They are left out because they have no name to count — that is a choice.
    • James and John are filtered by person: James the Less and John the Baptist do not count. A different identity rule moves the total.
    • The scope is the four Gospels, not the whole Bible. Widen the scope and the number changes.
    • Large books contain many named groups. Some of those groups will land on a number that already appears in the story.

    The claim is only this: under the rules above, the live count from this text is 153. You can reject the rules. You should not have to take the arithmetic on trust.

    John 21 says they caught 153 fish. Anyone can add 1 through 17 and get 153. The same chapter names five men in that boat. Count those five names through Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John — Peter, Thomas, Nathanael, James, and John — and the total is 153. The rules are on the page. The count is from the text, not from a quote. I am not asking you to believe a sermon. I am asking you to add, and then to read the chapter.
